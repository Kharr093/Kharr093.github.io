Kai Harris

Psychology📚

Hi! I’m a second-year Psychology student working toward a career in Forensic Psychology. My interest in this field comes down to a simple question that has always fascinated me: why do people do what they do? Long before starting my degree, I was drawn to true crime—not just for the dramatic stories, but because I wanted to understand the human behavior, motives, and psychological patterns behind them. Joining that curiosity with a formal education in psychology felt like the most natural path for me.

My journey into psychology is also deeply rooted in my everyday interactions. For years, I’ve been the person my friends turn to when they need to talk through personal problems. I’ve always naturally enjoyed listening, offering perspective, and helping people process what they’re going through. As much as I valued being a supportive friend, I realized I wanted more than just good intentions—I wanted the education, scientific framework, and professional language to truly make a difference in people's lives. That realization carries over into my personal life in unexpected ways. Outside of class, I spend a lot of time swimming and doing nails. While doing nails might seem unrelated to psychology on the surface, anyone who has sat through an appointment knows it almost always turns into a therapeutic venting session! I find that creating a comfortable environment allows people to open up naturally, reminding me of how much human connection matters.

In my coursework so far, I’ve been building a solid grounding in research, behavioral analysis, and human development. Forensic psychology sits right at the intersection of mental health and the legal system, which makes objective evaluation and genuine empathy equally important. As I finish out my second year, I’m focused on gaining practical experience in criminal justice, clinical, or research settings to prepare for graduate studies in forensic psychology down the line. I’m always open to connecting with fellow students, researchers, or professionals in the field, so feel free to reach out!
